PHASE ONE CURRICULUM

Build the foundation. Build the confidence.

Our first learning pathway focuses on desktop and laptop computers, moving from the basics toward practical skills adults can use every day.

HOW WE TEACH

We teach understanding, not memorization.

A computer can seem overwhelming when every icon, window, button, and message feels unfamiliar. We break that experience into understandable pieces and connect each new skill to something the learner already knows.

The curriculum is progressive, practical, and flexible. Learners can ask questions, repeat skills, and receive additional support when needed.

Platform note: Windows laptops and desktops are the best supported computers for Phase One. Chromebooks are also supported, particularly for ChromeOS, Google, browser, email, and other web based skills. Mac specific instruction is currently limited.

THE LEARNING PATH

From the first click to everyday confidence.

01

Computer Basics

Understand the computer, monitor, keyboard, mouse, desktop, icons, windows, and basic terminology.

  • Starting and shutting down
  • Keyboard and mouse
  • Desktop and icons
  • Windows and menus
02

Keyboard and Mouse

Build the physical skills needed to control the computer comfortably and accurately.

  • Clicking and double clicking
  • Right clicking
  • Scrolling
  • Dragging and dropping
  • Typing and keyboard keys
03

Files and Folders

Learn how digital information is organized and how to keep personal work easy to find.

  • Creating folders
  • Naming files
  • Saving and opening
  • Copying and moving
  • Downloads
04

Internet Basics

Learn how browsers and websites work and how to search for information with confidence.

  • Web browsers
  • Website addresses
  • Links and navigation
  • Internet searches
  • Downloads
05

Email and Communication

Practice the everyday communication skills that make email useful at home and in the community.

  • Reading and writing email
  • Replying and forwarding
  • Attachments
  • Downloading attachments
  • Organizing messages
06

Documents and Printing

Develop practical skills for creating, saving, editing, and printing simple documents.

  • Basic word processing
  • Typing and editing
  • Formatting text
  • Saving documents
  • Printing
07

Safety and Privacy

Recognize common online risks and learn habits that help protect accounts and personal information.

  • Strong passwords
  • Phishing
  • Scams
  • Safe downloads
  • Privacy
08

Everyday Computer Skills

Bring the pieces together for practical tasks learners may encounter in daily life.

  • Settings
  • Updates
  • Screenshots
  • USB devices
  • Basic troubleshooting

PRACTICE MATTERS

Your computer is part of the classroom.

Because learners bring their own computers, practice happens on the device they will actually use at home. Windows and ChromeOS can look and behave differently, and that is part of the learning process.

The instructor can help learners understand what is universal about computing and what is specific to their particular computer. Windows is the strongest supported platform for instruction, while Chromebook users can receive support with ChromeOS and Google based workflows.

PHASE TWO

Mobile technology comes later.

Smartphones and tablets have a different interface. They will receive their own learning opportunity after the desktop and laptop foundation is established.

Desktop and laptop first. Mobile second.
