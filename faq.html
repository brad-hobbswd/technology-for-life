FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

It is okay to ask.

Here are some of the questions learners may have before beginning Technology for Life.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Start with what you want to know.

Who are these classes for?

Technology for Life is designed for adult learners, including people who grew up before computers were part of everyday life and older adults who want to continue learning new technology skills.

Do I need computer experience?

No. Phase One is designed to establish the foundations, so you can begin even if you have very little or no computer experience.

How much do classes cost?

Classes are free. There is no tuition or class fee. Learners are encouraged to contribute through their time, attention, willingness to practice, patience, encouragement, or ability to help others. Financial donations are welcome when a learner is able, but no learner is required to donate money or volunteer in order to participate.

Are classes online?

Phase One classes are planned as in person training. The first learning pathway focuses on laptops. If a class is offered through Microsoft Teams, learners may participate from home and may use a desktop computer if that is the device they have.

What kind of computer should I bring?

A Windows laptop is the primary supported option for Phase One instruction. Chromebooks are also welcome, especially for ChromeOS, Google tools, web browsing, email, online safety, and other browser based skills. Mac laptops may be used, but Mac specific instruction is currently limited. Desktop computers are not permitted for in person training because they are not practical to transport. A desktop may be used from home if instruction is offered through Microsoft Teams.

Can I bring a Chromebook?

Yes. Chromebooks are welcome. The instructor is comfortable working with ChromeOS and Google based tools, and many foundational skills transfer well to Chromebook use.

Can I bring a Mac?

You may bring a Mac laptop, but Mac specific instruction is currently limited. Technology for Life's strongest hands on instruction is on Windows laptops, while Chromebook support is also available.

Can I bring a desktop computer?

Not for in person classes. Desktop computers are not practical to transport to a training location, so learners attending in person must bring a laptop. If instruction is offered through Microsoft Teams, a desktop computer may be used from home.

How long is each class?

Each weekly class meeting is designed to last two hours, including instruction, practice, questions, review, and individual support.

How long is a learning session?

A typical learning session is planned for at least six weeks, meeting one day each week for two hours. This is a minimum framework, not a rigid finish date. Some studies may take less time, while studies involving substantial hands on work, repetition, troubleshooting, or individual assistance may take longer.

What do I need to bring?

You need to bring your own Windows laptop or Chromebook for Phase One in person classes. Bringing your own device lets you practice on the computer you use at home. Desktop computers are not permitted for in person training.

Will Wi-Fi be available?

Wi-Fi will be available at the training location once an in person training site has been established.

What if I am afraid of making a mistake?

Mistakes are part of learning. The classroom is designed to give you a safe place to practice, ask questions, and try again.

What if everyone else learns faster than I do?

Learning is not a race. We make room for different learning speeds, repetition, questions, and individual assistance.

Will I be tested before I can attend?

No entrance test is required. You do not have to prove what you already know before you begin.

Will there be classes for phones and tablets?

Mobile devices are planned as a separate phase. Laptop fundamentals come first because they provide a strong foundation for understanding computing.

When will classes begin?

The first class dates, location, and registration details will be posted on the Schedule and Registration pages once they are established.

What if I have a question that is not listed here?

Please reach out. Questions are welcome, and you do not need to know the right computer terms to ask for help.

ONE MORE THING

You belong in the classroom.

You are never too old to learn something new, and you are never too inexperienced to begin.
